Why minimalist works

A minimalist resume removes everything that distracts from what you've done. No busy graphics, no rainbow of colours, no cramped sections — just clear typography and white space. Recruiters skim resumes in seconds, and a clean layout makes the important things easy to find. It also happens to be ATS-friendly by default, because there's nothing fancy for a parser to trip over.

What makes a layout minimalist

  • White space — generous margins and spacing between sections.
  • One clean font — a single readable typeface, maybe one weight for headings.
  • Restrained colour — usually black text with one subtle accent, at most.
  • Clear headings — simple, consistent section titles.
  • No clutter — no icons, photos or decorative lines unless they truly help.

Who should use a minimalist resume

It suits almost every role, and it's especially strong for corporate, finance, consulting, engineering, law and academic applications, where substance outweighs flair. If you're unsure which style to pick, minimalist is the safest default — it never looks out of place.

Frequently asked questions

What is a minimalist resume template?

A minimalist resume uses a clean, simple layout — plenty of white space, one or two fonts, minimal colour and no graphics — so the focus stays on your content. It reads well and is almost always ATS-friendly.

Is a minimalist resume good for ATS?

Yes — minimalist layouts are among the safest for applicant tracking systems because they avoid graphics, columns-via-tables and decorative elements that parsers can misread. Clean text and clear headings parse reliably.

Who should use a minimalist resume?

Almost anyone, but it's especially good for corporate, finance, consulting, engineering and academic roles where substance matters more than visual flair. It also suits anyone who wants a timeless, no-fuss look.

What makes a resume look minimalist?

Generous white space, a single clean font, limited colour (often just black and one accent), clear section headings, and no icons or heavy graphics. Restraint is the whole point.
