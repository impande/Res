You don't need work experience to write a great resume

For your first job, recruiters don't expect a long career history. They look for a clear, well-organised one-page resume that shows you can learn, finish things and work with people. Fill the space usually taken by work experience with what you do have: education, projects, internships, part-time work, volunteering, certifications and skills.

Step-by-step: how to write a resume for your first job

Step 1: Put your contact details at the top

Full name (largest text on the page), phone number, a professional email (firstname.lastname@…), your city, and a LinkedIn profile. Add GitHub, Behance or a portfolio link if it shows your work. Leave out your photo, date of birth, marital status and full address unless the employer asks for them.

Step 2: Write a two-line career objective

Name the role you want, then the two or three strongest things you bring. Keep it specific — generic lines like "seeking a challenging position" are skipped by every recruiter. Examples:

  • Computer Science graduate seeking a junior developer role; built 3 full-stack projects in React and Node.js and solved 300+ DSA problems.
  • B.Com graduate looking for an accounts executive role, skilled in Tally Prime, GST returns and advanced Excel.
  • Recent BBA graduate seeking a sales trainee role; led college fest sponsorships that raised ₹1.2 lakh.
  • Customer-focused student looking for a part-time retail job; reliable, quick to learn and available on weekends.

More examples by field: career objectives for freshers.

Step 3: Lead with your education

As a fresher, education is your strongest section, so it goes first. Include your degree, college, graduation year and CGPA or percentage if it's 7+/10 or 65%+. Add relevant coursework, a final-year project title or academic honours. For 12th and 10th, one line each is enough — only if you're a recent graduate.

Step 4: Replace work experience with projects, internships and part-time work

This is the section that wins interviews. For each project, internship, part-time job or volunteering role, write 2–3 bullet points that show what you did and what happened as a result. Use numbers wherever you can.

WeakWorked on a college project about a library system.
StrongBuilt a library management app in Java and MySQL for 1,200 students, cutting book-issue time from 5 minutes to under 1.
WeakDid an internship in marketing.
StrongPlanned 24 Instagram posts in a 6-week internship, growing followers by 38% and engagement by 2.1×.
WeakHelped at my uncle's shop.
StrongHandled billing and stock for a family grocery store on weekends, serving 80+ customers a day and keeping cash counts error-free.

Step 5: List skills that match the job

Read the job post and list the tools and skills it asks for that you genuinely have — this is also how applicant tracking systems (ATS) match your resume. Mix hard skills (Excel, Python, Tally, Canva, SQL) with 3–4 soft skills you can back up (communication, teamwork, problem-solving). See the full list: skills to put on a resume.

Step 6: Add certifications and achievements

Online courses (Google, Coursera, NPTEL, Microsoft), hackathons, competitions, college clubs, NSS/NCC, sports captaincy, or a leadership role in a fest. One line each, with the result if there is one. A short hobbies line is optional — only if it says something useful about you.

Step 7: Use a one-page ATS-friendly format and proofread

Choose a clean single-column template, keep it to one page, use one font at 10–12pt, and check spelling twice (ask a friend too). Save it as a PDF named Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf. Before you apply, run it through a free ATS score checker.

Making a resume for your first job while still in school

Applying for a part-time, weekend or summer job (retail, café, tutoring, delivery)? Keep it simple:

  • Objective: the job you want and your availability (e.g. "available evenings and weekends").
  • Education: your school, class/grade and any strong marks or awards.
  • Experience: babysitting, tutoring younger students, helping in a family business, volunteering, school events.
  • Skills: customer service, cash handling, punctuality, teamwork, languages you speak.
  • Activities: sports teams, clubs, student council — they show responsibility.

Common first-resume mistakes to avoid

  • Going over one page. Cut anything that doesn't support the job you want.
  • Listing duties, not results. "Responsible for…" tells nothing — show what changed because of you.
  • A generic objective. Name the role and your top skills.
  • Fancy designs with tables, columns and graphics that ATS software can't read.
  • Typos and mixed formatting — the most common reason freshers are rejected.
  • An unprofessional email address — use a simple name-based one.
  • One resume for every job. Tweak your objective and skills for each application.

Resume format after your first job

Once you've worked for 6–12 months, your resume should change shape. Recruiters now care most about what you did at work:

  • Move Work Experience above Education. Your job is now your strongest proof.
  • Replace the objective with a 2–3 line professional summary: your role, years of experience, top skills and one result.
  • Write 3–5 achievement bullets for your job, with numbers (time saved, sales, accuracy, customers served).
  • Shorten projects to the one or two that are still relevant, and remove school (10th/12th) details.
  • Keep it one page until you have about 5 years of experience.

New order after your first job: Contact → Summary → Work Experience → Skills → Education → Certifications.

Frequently asked questions

What do I put on a resume for my first job with no experience?

Education first, then academic projects, internships, part-time or volunteer work, skills, certifications and achievements. Give each project or role 2–3 bullet points that show what you did and the result.

How long should a first job resume be?

One page. Freshers rarely need more, and recruiters spend only seconds on a first scan.

What is the best resume format for a first job?

Reverse-chronological and single-column: contact, objective, education, projects/internships, skills, certifications. Avoid tables and graphics that ATS software can't read.

Should a first job resume have an objective or a summary?

A two-line career objective — the role you want and your top skills, e.g. "Commerce graduate seeking an accounts executive role, skilled in Tally, GST filing and Excel."

How do I change my resume format after my first job?

Move Work Experience above Education, swap the objective for a short professional summary, write results-focused bullets for your job, and trim projects to the most relevant one or two.

Can I make a resume for my first job while still in school?

Yes. Lead with your school, add part-time work, tutoring, family-business help, volunteering, clubs and sports, and list skills like customer service, punctuality and teamwork.

Should I add a photo or date of birth?

Not unless the employer asks. Most private and international employers prefer resumes without a photo, age or marital status. Some government jobs in India ask for a biodata instead.

Which file format should I send?

PDF, unless the job post asks for Word — it looks the same on every device. Name it Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf.
