Declaration in Resume

Short answer: a resume declaration is a one-line statement at the very end confirming your details are true, followed by place, date and signature. It's a traditional convention in India — common for government and fresher roles, optional for modern private-sector jobs.

What is a declaration — and do you need one?

The declaration is the short closing line that says your resume's information is accurate. It's a long-standing convention in India, still expected for government, banking, teaching and many fresher applications. For private companies, startups and international roles, it's usually optional — recruiters there rarely look for it, and the space is often better used for skills or achievements. If in doubt for a traditional role, include it; otherwise it's fine to leave out.

Correct declaration format

Keep it to one clean line, then place, date and signature at the bottom:

Declaration
I hereby declare that the above information is true and correct to the best of my knowledge and belief.
 
Place: Pune  ·  Date: 14/10/2026  ·  (Signature) Priya Sharma

6 sample declaration lines you can use

  • I hereby declare that the above information is true and correct to the best of my knowledge and belief.
  • I solemnly declare that all the details furnished above are true to the best of my knowledge.
  • I declare that the information provided in this resume is accurate and complete.
  • I hereby affirm that the particulars mentioned above are correct and truly stated.
  • The above-mentioned information is true to the best of my knowledge and belief.
  • I confirm that the details given in this resume are genuine and accurate.

Where to place it

The declaration goes at the very end of your resume, after every other section. Put the place and date on the left and your signature or typed name on the right. On a digital PDF, a typed name is perfectly acceptable in place of a handwritten signature.

Should you include it?

  • Include for government, PSU, banking, teaching and traditional/fresher applications.
  • Optional / skip for private companies, startups, IT and international roles — use the space for skills or projects instead.

Frequently asked questions

What is a declaration in a resume?

A declaration is a short statement at the end of a resume confirming that all the information provided is true and correct to the best of your knowledge, usually followed by the place, date and your signature.

Is a declaration necessary in a resume?

Not always. It's a traditional convention still common in India, especially for government, banking and fresher roles. For modern private-sector, startup and international roles it's usually optional and often left out to save space.

What is the correct format of a declaration?

A single line such as: 'I hereby declare that the above information is true and correct to the best of my knowledge and belief.' Follow it with Place, Date and your Signature/Name, aligned at the bottom.

Where should the declaration be placed in a resume?

At the very end of the resume, after all other sections, with the place and date on the left and your signature or name on the right.

Should freshers add a declaration?

It's optional. Many Indian freshers include it for traditional and government applications; for private companies you can safely leave it out and use the space for skills or projects.
