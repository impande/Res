What an ATS score actually measures

Most companies run resumes through an applicant tracking system (ATS) before a human ever sees them. The ATS parses your resume, checks it against the job's requirements, and ranks it. An ATS score estimates how well your resume survives that process — based on keyword match, formatting the parser can read, standard section structure, and whether your content reads as relevant and achievement-focused.

A low score usually isn't because you're underqualified — it's because the resume is formatted or worded in a way the system can't read or rank well. That's fixable, and that's exactly what this checker is for.

How this ATS checker scores your resume

Unlike tools that just spit out a number, this checker grades the things screening systems and recruiters care about, in seven areas, and tells you why you lost points:

  • Parseability & contact — standard headings, clean single-column text, full contact details.
  • Structure — clear Summary, Experience, Education and Skills sections with consistent dates.
  • Impact — how many bullets carry real numbers and outcomes (the heaviest weight).
  • Action verbs & clarity — strong, concise bullets with no filler.
  • Skills & keywords — role-relevant skills that also appear in your bullets.
  • Experience depth and length — 1 page early in your career, up to 2 pages after 10+ years.

Your overall score is a fixed weighted average of those areas, so the same resume gets the same score every time. Resumes over three pages, or without achievement bullets, are capped — exactly the resumes that struggle in real screening.

How to improve your ATS score

  • Match the job's keywords — mirror the exact skills and terms from the job description (without stuffing).
  • Use a clean, single-column layout — avoid text boxes, tables and graphics that parsers misread.
  • Keep standard headings — "Work Experience", "Education", "Skills" parse reliably.
  • Write achievements, not duties — quantify results ("cut costs 20%") instead of listing tasks.
  • Keep it tight — 1–2 pages, no filler; length itself can drag the score down.
  • Export a text-based PDF — not a scanned image or screenshot.

The checker tells you which of these is costing you points, so you fix the right things first.
