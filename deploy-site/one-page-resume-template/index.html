Short answer: for most candidates (under ~10 years of experience), a single clean page is ideal. Fit it by keeping a tight summary, your most relevant roles, concise one-line bullets, and adjusting margins and spacing before shrinking the font.

Why one page is usually right

Recruiters spend seconds on each resume. A single, well-edited page forces you to include only your strongest, most relevant content — which is exactly what gets you shortlisted. Students, freshers and most professionals should aim for one page. Two pages are fine only when you genuinely have a long, senior career with relevant detail to justify it.

How to fit everything on one page

  • Tighten the summary — 2–3 sharp lines, not a paragraph.
  • Prioritise roles — recent and relevant first; trim or summarise the rest.
  • Concise bullets — 3–4 per role, one line each, each with a result.
  • Adjust layout first — margins and spacing before font size; never shrink text below readable.
  • Cut filler — drop generic objectives, obvious skills and unrelated detail.

What to keep (and what to cut)

Keep
Summary · recent relevant experience · key skills · education · strongest achievements
Cut or shorten
Old/unrelated jobs · generic objective · obvious skills · long paragraphs · references ("available on request")

Frequently asked questions

Should my resume be one page?

For most people — students, freshers and professionals with under ~10 years of experience — yes, one page is ideal. It forces you to keep only your strongest, most relevant content. Two pages are acceptable for long or senior careers.

How do I fit my resume on one page?

Keep a tight summary, show only your most recent and relevant roles, use concise one-line bullets, and tighten margins and spacing before shrinking the font. A one-page template does most of this for you.

Is a one-page resume ATS-friendly?

Yes — length doesn't affect ATS parsing. What matters is clean formatting and clear headings. A well-built one-page template is fully ATS-readable.

What if I have too much to fit on one page?

Prioritise. Trim older or unrelated roles to a single line, remove filler, and keep only achievements that matter for the job you're targeting. If you genuinely have 10+ years of relevant experience, a second page is fine.
