CV or resume — what freshers should know

In India, "CV" and "resume" are used interchangeably for most job applications, so don't overthink the label. Strictly, a resume is a short, tailored, one-page summary, while a CV is a longer, detailed academic and professional record used for research, academia and some international roles. As a fresher applying to companies, keep it concise and well-structured — that's what recruiters want either way.

The right CV format for freshers, section by section

  1. Header — name, professional email, phone, city, and one link (LinkedIn or portfolio).
  2. Objective or summary — two lines on the role you want and your strengths.
  3. Education — degree, institution, year, and strong grades or coursework. Comes first for freshers.
  4. Projects & internships — your stand-in for work experience, each with 2–3 result-focused bullets.
  5. Skills — relevant technical and soft skills.
  6. Certifications — online courses and training tied to the role.
  7. Optional — achievements, positions of responsibility, languages.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a CV and a resume?

In most of India the words are used interchangeably for job applications. Strictly, a resume is a short, 1-page summary tailored to one job, while a CV (curriculum vitae) is a longer, detailed record of your full academic and professional history — used more for academic, research and some international roles. For a fresher applying to companies, a concise 1-page document is best, whatever you call it.

What is the correct CV format for freshers?

Header and contact details, a two-line objective, education (first), projects and internships, skills, certifications, and optional sections — in a clean, single-column, ATS-friendly layout.

How many pages should a fresher CV be?

One page is ideal; two at most. A fresher rarely has enough relevant content to justify more, and recruiters prefer concise documents.

Should a fresher CV have a photo?
