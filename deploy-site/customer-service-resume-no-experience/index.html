Customer service is a skill you've already practised

Banks, e-commerce companies, telecom providers, hotels, hospitals, airlines, SaaS start-ups and retail chains all hire customer service and customer support executives straight out of college. They don't expect you to know their software — they train you. What they can't train quickly is how you treat people: whether you stay calm, listen properly, explain clearly and follow up. Your resume has to show that, using examples from your own life.

What hiring managers look for in a customer service fresher

  • Clear communication — spoken for phone and counter roles, written for chat and email. A tidy, error-free resume is your first proof.
  • Patience and empathy — evidence you've handled an upset customer, parent or classmate without losing your cool.
  • Problem-solving within a process — fixing the issue, not just apologising, while following rules on refunds and escalations.
  • Languages — English plus Hindi or a regional language widens the teams you can join.
  • Tool comfort — typing speed, Excel or Sheets, and any CRM or helpdesk you've seen.
  • Availability — weekends, holidays or rotational shifts, if you can genuinely do them.

Step-by-step: what to write on a customer service resume

Seven steps, in the order they appear on the page.

Step 1: Put contact details and languages up front

Name, phone, a professional email, city and LinkedIn. Recruiters often filter by language, so a headline like Customer Support Executive | English, Hindi, Malayalam | Chat & Email works well. More ideas: resume headlines for freshers.

Step 2: Write an objective that names the channel

“Customer service” covers very different jobs, so say which one you want. Examples:

  • BA English graduate seeking a chat and email support role; types 45 WPM, writes clear, friendly replies and speaks English, Hindi and Malayalam.
  • B.Com fresher looking for a customer service role in banking; served 60+ walk-in customers a day at my family's pharmacy counter.
  • Hotel management graduate seeking a front-desk or guest relations role after 4 months of industrial training in a hotel front office.
  • Process-driven graduate seeking an e-commerce support role; handled orders, returns and complaints for 200+ buyers of my own Instagram store.

Prefer a summary? See resume summary for freshers.

Step 3: Keep education short

Degree, college, year and percentage or CGPA if it helps. Add one line of relevant coursework — business communication, English, psychology, hospitality or marketing. Most support roles accept any graduate, and some accept 12th pass, so don't worry if your degree isn't “related”.

Step 4: Turn customer-facing moments into experience

Create an Experience section from anything where you dealt with real people and their problems:

  • Part-time retail, café, cinema or delivery work.
  • The counter at a family shop, pharmacy or clinic.
  • An Instagram, Meesho or WhatsApp store — DMs, payments, returns.
  • Registration or help desks at college fests, admissions or events.
  • Tutoring, NSS or NGO volunteering, or a helpline.

For chat and email roles, attach a short writing sample — three replies to imaginary complaints (late delivery, wrong item, refund delay). Hiring managers rarely see freshers do this.

Step 5: List the skills support teams use

  • Soft skills: active listening, empathy, calm under pressure, clear explanation, de-escalation, ownership and follow-up.
  • Hard skills: typing speed (e.g. 45 WPM), email and chat etiquette, MS Excel or Google Sheets, helpdesk tools such as Freshdesk, Zendesk or Zoho Desk (free trials and tutorials count as basics), order-tracking and billing software.

Learn the vocabulary — CSAT, first response time, resolution time, escalation, ticket — so you can use it in interviews. More: skills to put on a resume and strengths in resume.

Step 6: Add certifications and languages

Useful, mostly free options: customer service courses on HubSpot Academy or Freshworks Academy, a typing certificate, a spoken English or business English course, or a Skill India customer care course. Then a Languages line — each one you can genuinely handle a customer conversation in.

Step 7: Format it cleanly and check your writing

One page, one column, no typos. Read it aloud — if a sentence sounds stiff, it'll read stiff in a chat window too. Save as PDF and check it with the free ATS score checker before uploading to job portals.

Customer service resume mistakes to avoid

  • Claiming “excellent communication skills” with no example. Show it with a bullet.
  • Spelling or grammar errors — fatal for chat and email roles.
  • Ignoring languages. A second or third language can decide which team hires you.
  • Inventing CSAT scores or call numbers you can't explain in an interview.
  • Leaving out shift or weekend availability when the job post asks for it.
  • Copying one generic resume for in-store, phone and chat roles.

Frequently asked questions

How do I write a customer service resume with no experience?

Lead with a two-line objective naming the support role, then education, then an Experience section built from customer-facing moments: part-time retail, a family shop counter, an online store, fest help desks or tutoring. Add skills, certifications and the languages you can use with customers.

What skills should I put on a customer service resume?

Active listening, empathy, clear spoken and written communication, problem-solving and de-escalation, plus typing speed, email and chat etiquette, Excel or Google Sheets, and basics of a helpdesk tool such as Freshdesk, Zendesk or Zoho Desk.

What is a good objective for a customer support executive fresher?

Name the role and channel, then your strongest proof. For example: BA graduate seeking a chat and email support role; types 45 WPM, writes clear replies and speaks English, Hindi and Malayalam.

Is customer service the same as a BPO job?

They overlap. Customer service is the work; a BPO does it for client companies, while banks, e-commerce firms, hotels and retail chains also hire their own in-house support teams. The resume is similar, but BPO applications put more weight on shifts, voice or non-voice process and communication tests.

Do I need typing speed for a customer support job?

For chat and email support, yes — mention your speed and accuracy, for example 45 WPM at 97%. For phone or in-store roles it is helpful but less important than spoken communication.

Should I mention CSAT or other metrics as a fresher?

Only real numbers you can explain, such as customers served per shift or your store's feedback rating. Learn terms like CSAT, first response time and resolution time for interviews, but do not invent figures.

Which languages should I list?

Every language you can comfortably hold a customer conversation in, with a level such as fluent or conversational. Regional languages are an advantage for domestic support teams.
