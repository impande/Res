What a resume headline is (and where it goes)

A resume headline is the short, bold line right under your name — and the "Resume Headline" field on job portals like Naukri. It's the first thing a recruiter reads, so it has to label you clearly and make them want to read on. Don't confuse it with a summary (2–3 lines) or objective (what you want) — the headline is a single, punchy line.

How to write a strong headline (the formula)

[Qualification] + [top skill(s)] + [target role]. Keep it specific and under ~15 words. Use real skills, not adjectives like "hardworking".

Frequently asked questions

What is a resume headline for a fresher?

A resume headline is a short, one-line phrase at the top of your resume (or in your Naukri profile) that sums up who you are professionally — your qualification plus a key skill or target role. Example: 'Computer Science Graduate skilled in Java and Web Development.'

What is the difference between a resume headline and a summary?

A headline is a single line that labels you; a summary is 2–3 lines that expand on your strengths. The headline grabs attention; the summary backs it up. See our resume summary for freshers guide.

What should a fresher write in the Naukri resume headline?

Combine your qualification, a top skill or two, and the role you want: 'B.Tech (IT) Fresher | Skilled in Python & SQL | Seeking Software Developer Role.' Keep it under about 15 words.

How long should a resume headline be?

One line — roughly 8–15 words. It should be readable at a glance.
